Conducting thorough background checks is essential for any organization working with children or vulnerable populations. Here's your complete guide to volunteer background check best practices.

## Types of Background Checks

### 1. Criminal Background Check
The foundation of volunteer screening. This typically includes:

- **State criminal history**: Checks for convictions in your state
- **National criminal database**: Broader search across multiple states
- **Sex offender registry**: Critical for organizations working with children

**Recommendation**: Required for all volunteers with direct contact with children.

### 2. Child Abuse Clearance
Many states require specific child abuse registry checks. These search state databases for:

- Substantiated child abuse reports
- Individuals barred from working with children
- Pending investigations

**Recommendation**: Required in most states for youth-serving organizations.

### 3. FBI Fingerprint Check
The most comprehensive background check available:

- Federal criminal history
- Multi-state criminal records
- Most thorough screening option

**Recommendation**: Required for certain positions (coaches, overnight supervision) and in some states.

### 4. Reference Checks
Don't overlook the human element:

- Contact previous volunteer coordinators
- Verify work history with children
- Ask specific questions about behavior and reliability

**Recommendation**: Conduct for all volunteers, especially those in leadership roles.

## How Often Should You Renew?

### Standard Renewal Periods

Most organizations follow these timelines:

- **Criminal background checks**: Every 3-5 years
- **Child abuse clearances**: Annually or every 2 years (varies by state)
- **FBI fingerprint checks**: Every 5 years
- **Reference checks**: With each new role or program

### State-Specific Requirements

Requirements vary significantly by state. For example:

- **Pennsylvania**: Requires three clearances renewed every 5 years
- **California**: Requires DOJ clearance every 5 years
- **New York**: Requires clearance every 3 years

**Action Item**: Research your state's specific requirements and set up a tracking system.

## Creating a Screening Policy

### Essential Policy Components

Your volunteer screening policy should include:

1. **Clear requirements**: What checks are needed for which roles
2. **Timeline expectations**: How long the process takes
3. **Cost responsibility**: Who pays for clearances
4. **Disqualifying offenses**: What prevents someone from volunteering
5. **Appeal process**: How volunteers can address concerns
6. **Confidentiality**: How information is stored and protected

### Sample Policy Framework

```
All volunteers with direct contact with children must complete:
- State criminal background check
- Child abuse clearance
- Two professional references

Clearances must be:
- Dated within the past 60 days at time of submission
- Renewed every 3 years
- Kept on file with the organization

Disqualifying offenses include:
- Any conviction involving children
- Violent crimes
- Sexual offenses
- [Add organization-specific criteria]
```

## Red Flags to Watch For

### During the Application Process

- Reluctance to complete background checks
- Gaps in volunteer/work history
- Inconsistent information
- Pressure to skip screening steps

### In Background Check Results

- Convictions involving violence
- Any offenses against children
- Pattern of dishonesty (fraud, theft)
- Recent serious offenses

### Important Note
Always consult with legal counsel about how to handle concerning results. Fair hiring laws apply to volunteers in many jurisdictions.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Inconsistent Application
**Mistake**: Only screening some volunteers

**Solution**: Apply screening consistently to all volunteers in similar roles

### 2. Expired Clearances
**Mistake**: Letting clearances lapse without renewal

**Solution**: Set up automatic reminders 60-90 days before expiration

### 3. Poor Record Keeping
**Mistake**: Losing track of who has current clearances

**Solution**: Use a centralized tracking system (like VolunteerClear!)

### 4. Inadequate Security
**Mistake**: Storing sensitive documents in unlocked cabinets

**Solution**: Use secure, encrypted storage with limited access

### 5. Ignoring State Differences
**Mistake**: Using the same process for volunteers in different states

**Solution**: Research and comply with each state's requirements

## Compliance Checklist

Use this checklist to ensure your screening process is compliant:

- [ ] Research state-specific requirements
- [ ] Create written screening policy
- [ ] Obtain volunteer consent for checks
- [ ] Use reputable background check providers
- [ ] Store documents securely
- [ ] Set up renewal tracking system
- [ ] Train staff on screening procedures
- [ ] Review policy annually
- [ ] Document all screening decisions
- [ ] Maintain confidentiality
